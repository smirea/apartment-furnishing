UltraLight Slat at a glance
Every finish shares the same build, so one column covers the line. Slat width, groove and the
panel joint never change; only the veneer and the felt colour do.

 US E & P E R FO R MA N C E

 Flexibility                          The coreless build lets a panel follow a curved wall, a column or an arch. Bend it across the slats, never

How many boxes?
Panels are sold by the box, so work in boxes. Divide the wall width by what one box covers,
round up, then pick the length that suits the wall height.

Start at a corner
Corners are what people look at, so start at one — inside or outside, whichever end of the wall is most
visible. Work away from it and let the cut panel land at the far end, ideally the end that dies into another
corner, a cabinet or trim. UltraLight panels are symmetrical end to end, so there is no right way round and
nothing to orient: every end carries the same half-groove. Cut the last panel down the middle of a groove
and its new end matches the factory one.

   No acclimation needed. There is no wood or MDF core to swell or shrink, so you can install the day the boxes arrive. Do still open
   several boxes and lay panels out before fixing — real veneer varies, and blending across the whole wall stops a patch showing
   where one box ends.

  1      Prep the wall
         Start with a clean, dry, flat wall. Take off outlet covers and switch plates and pull any nails or hooks. Find and
         mark the studs if you’re using screws. The panel is 9mm and flexible, so it follows the wall rather than
         straightening it — fill or sand anything that would telegraph through.

  2      Mark your layout
         Start at the corner shown on page 4. Draw a plumb line on the first panel’s leading edge — corners are rarely
         plumb, so trust the line, not the wall. Everything else follows it, so check it twice.

  3      There is no wrong way round
         Both ends of an UltraLight panel are identical: each carries half a v-groove. There is no lip to lead with and no
         overhang to tuck under. Butt the next panel straight up against the last one and the two half-grooves close into
         a full groove that matches every other groove on the wall. Push until the chamfers seat — the 45° faces locate
         each other.

         check plumb every two or three panels.

  7      Openings & finishing
         Hold the panel up, mark the outlet or switch on its face, then cut the opening before you fix the panel. A jigsaw, a
         multi-tool or a sharp utility knife all work — the panel is 9mm of veneer and felt with nothing hard inside, and the
         cover plate hides the cut edges. At 0.35″ of added depth a standard plate usually still sits flat. A cut end shows
         the felt edge rather than end grain, so plan cuts to land behind baseboard, at the ceiling, or into a corner.

  8      Corners and edges
         Every panel end is half a v-groove — which makes it a 45° chamfer running the full length of the panel. That one
         fact handles both corners: at an inside corner the two chamfers face each other and mitre themselves, with
         nothing to cut; at an outside corner the turning panel drops until its top side is flush with the wall, which lines the
         two chamfers up into one continuous bevel with nothing overhanging.
