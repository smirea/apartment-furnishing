How many boxes?
Panels are sold by the box, so work in boxes. Divide the wall width by what one box covers,
round up, then pick the length that suits the wall height.

Start at a corner
Corners are what people look at, so start at one — inside or outside, whichever end of the wall is most
visible. Work away from it and let the cut panel land at the far end, ideally the end that dies into another
corner, a cabinet or trim. Turn the first panel so its overhanging slat edge sits in the corner and the felt lip
faces the run, which is the same “lead with the lip” rule you follow across the whole wall (page 5). The lap
joint puts every seam at the same 0.41″ gap as the slats, so the joins themselves never show.

   Real veneer varies. No two slats are alike. Open several boxes and lay panels out before fixing, so the tone and grain blend across
   the whole wall and there’s no patch where one box ends. Unbox the panels and lay them flat on the floor of the room they’re going
   into, out of their packaging, for 48 to 72 hours before installing, so the wood can adjust to the room’s humidity. Keep them clear of
   direct sun and heat sources while they settle. Don’t stand them on end against a wall, as that can make them bow. If one has a
   slight bow, lay it high side up with a board and a few books on top while it settles.

  1      Prep the wall
         Start with a clean, dry, flat wall. Take off outlet covers and switch plates and pull any nails or hooks. Find and
         mark the studs if you're using screws.

  2      Mark your layout
         Start at the corner shown on page 4. Draw a plumb line on the first panel’s leading edge — corners are rarely
         plumb, so trust the line, not the wall. Everything else follows it, so check it twice.

  3      Lead with the lip
         Each panel has a bare felt lip on one edge and an overhanging slat on the other. Turn every panel so the lip faces
         the direction you're working. Then each new panel's overhanging slat drops straight over the lip of the panel
         before it, with nothing to tuck under. The panels are symmetrical top to bottom, so you can flip them end for end
         to face either way.

  7      Openings & finishing
         Hold the panel up, mark the outlet or switch on its face, then cut the opening before you fix the panel. A jigsaw or
         multi-tool goes through the slats; a utility knife handles the felt between them. The cover plate hides the cut
         edges. The panel adds only 0.39″ of depth, so a standard cover plate usually still sits flat — check before you cut.
         The slats are veneered on the face and both edges, so a factory edge looks finished — but a cut end shows the
         rubberwood core. Plan cuts to land where they don’t show: behind baseboard at the floor, at the ceiling, or into
         a corner.

  8      Corners, edges and trim
         Every slat is its own piece, stapled to the felt from behind every 10–13″, so a slat lifts off cleanly and can be
         reused as trim. Butt two slats together to wrap an outside corner. An edge that ends with the slat overhang
         arrives finished as it is; an edge that ends with the felt lip takes one slat laid flat on the lip, flush with the felt edge.
         Same veneer, same finish, nothing to colour-match — and no rip cuts.
         At an outside corner both panels present the same 15.5 mm felt lip and the two trim slats butt over those lips,
         one slat’s end flush with the other’s face. Each lands 5 mm on its lip and hangs 22 mm past it, leaving the usual
         10.5 mm gap before the first slat. Because the panel is only 10 mm deep, both panels come back from the corner
         to make that work — 17 mm on the lapped side, 12 mm on the other. Glue and pin the two slats.
         Inside corners need no trim. Either lap the next panel’s overhanging slat over the first panel’s felt lip — the same
         seam you have everywhere else — or butt them. The felts meet edge to face and never overlap: one runs into the
         corner, the other stops a felt thickness short of it. Nothing collides at 10 mm deep, so neither panel is held back.
         On the panel whose felt runs in, the exposed lip comes out at 10.5 mm — the same as every other gap; the other
         wall shows its full 15.5 mm lip. Turn the wider one to the less visible wall, or use the lap option, which keeps both
         walls at the usual gap.
