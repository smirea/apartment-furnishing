How many boxes?
Panels are sold by the box, so work in boxes. Divide the wall width by what one box covers,
round up, then pick the length that suits the wall height.

Start at a corner
Corners are what people look at, so start at one — inside or outside, whichever end of the wall is most visible. Work
away from it and let the cut panel land at the far end — ideally the end that dies into another corner, a cabinet or trim.
Turn the first panel so its overhanging slat edge sits in the corner and the felt lip faces the run, which is the same “lead
with the lip” rule you follow across the whole wall (page 6). The lap joint puts every seam at the same 0.41″ gap as the
slats, so the joins themselves never show.

   Solid wood varies. No two slats are alike. Open several boxes and lay panels out before fixing, so the tone and grain blend across
   the whole wall and there's no patch where one box ends. Unbox the panels and lay them flat on the floor of the room they're going
   into, out of their packaging, for 48 to 72 hours before installing, so the wood can adjust to the room's humidity. Keep them clear of
   direct sun and heat sources while they settle. Don't stand them on end against a wall, as that can make them bow. If one has a
   slight bow, lay it high side up with a board and a few books on top while it settles.

  1      Prep the wall
         Start with a clean, dry, flat wall. Take off outlet covers and switch plates and pull any nails or hooks. Find and
         mark the studs if you're using screws.

  2      Mark your layout
         Start at the corner shown on page 5. Draw a plumb line on the first panel’s leading edge — corners are rarely
         plumb, so trust the line, not the wall. Everything else follows it, so check it twice.

  3      Lead with the lip
         Each panel has a bare felt lip on one edge and an overhanging slat on the other. Turn every panel so the lip faces
         the direction you're working. Then each new panel's overhanging slat drops straight over the lip of the panel
         before it, with nothing to tuck under. The panels are symmetrical top to bottom, so you can flip them end for end
         to face either way.

  5      Work across the wall
         Lower each new panel so its overhanging slat sits over the previous panel's lip and the two felt edges butt flush.
         The seam should show the same gap as the rest of the wall: 0.41″ on Oak and Walnut, 0.5″ on Bamboo. Fix the
         panel, then check plumb every two or three panels.

   If a slat bows. Solid wood can bow slightly as it adjusts to a new room. Pull it flat with construction adhesive behind the panel and
   a trim-head screw through the felt gap beside the bowed slat, into a stud or solid backing. Brace it while the adhesive cures, and
   colour the screw head with a black marker to hide it. Save your flattest panels for the most visible spots.

  7      Openings & finishing
         Hold the panel up, mark the outlet or switch on its face, then cut the opening before you fix the panel. A jigsaw or
         multi-tool goes through the slats; a utility knife handles the felt between them. The cover plate hides the cut
         edges. The panel adds about 0.87″ of depth, so fit box extenders where a device now sits proud of the wall.
         Cut ends and edges are solid wood all the way through, so they look finished with no core showing.

  8      Corners, edges and trim
         Every slat is its own piece, stapled to the felt from behind every 10–13″, so a slat lifts off cleanly and can be
         reused as trim. Butt two slats together to wrap an outside corner, or run a single slat down an exposed edge — it
         caps that edge whether you turn the felt lip or the overhanging slat outward. Same wood, same finish, nothing to
         colour-match.
         At an outside corner both panels present the same 15.5 mm felt lip and the two trim slats butt over those lips,
         one slat’s end flush with the other’s face. Each lands 5 mm on its lip and hangs 22 mm past it, leaving the usual
         10.5 mm gap before the first slat; hold the second panel back 13 mm so its lip lands in the same place. Glue and
         pin the two slats.
         Inside corners need no trim. Either lap the next panel’s overhanging slat over the first panel’s felt lip — the same
         seam you have everywhere else — or butt the two panels and leave a small open square that reads as a shadow
         line. On the lap option the slat reaches 22 mm in, so hold that panel back 6.5 mm and the two meet tight.
