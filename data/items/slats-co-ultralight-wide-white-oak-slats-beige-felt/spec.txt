UltraLight Wide
White Oak · Walnut · Dark Smoke Oak
Real wood veneer laminated straight onto 9mm recycled acoustic felt — no
MDF, no hardwood, no rigid core of any kind. That is what makes it 9mm deep,
light enough to hang solo, and flexible enough to follow a gentle curve. Three
broad 3.82″ faces per panel, divided by 3mm shadow reveals. This guide covers
what an installer or specifier needs to measure a wall, price the job and put it
up.

UltraLight Wide at a glance

How many boxes?
Panels are sold by the box, so work in boxes. Divide the wall width by what one box covers,
round up, then pick the length that suits the wall height.

Start at a corner
Corners are what people look at, so start at one — inside or outside, whichever end of the wall is most
visible. Work away from it and let the cut panel land at the far end, ideally the end that dies into another
corner, a cabinet or trim. UltraLight panels are symmetrical end to end, so there is no right way round and
nothing to orient: every end carries the same half-groove. Cut the last panel down the middle of a groove
and its new end matches the factory one.

   No acclimation needed. There is no wood or MDF core to swell or shrink, so you can install the day the boxes arrive. Do still open
   several boxes and lay panels out before fixing — real veneer varies, and blending across the whole wall stops a patch showing
   where one box ends.

  1      Prep the wall
         Start with a clean, dry, flat wall. Take off outlet covers and switch plates and pull any nails or hooks. The panel is
         9mm and flexible, so it follows the wall rather than straightening it — fill or sand anything that would telegraph
         through. Adhesive needs a sound surface: scrape loose paint and let fresh plaster cure.

  2      Mark your layout
         Start at the corner shown on page 4. Draw a plumb line on the first panel’s leading edge — corners are rarely
         plumb, so trust the line, not the wall. Everything else follows it, so check it twice.

  3      There is no wrong way round
         Both ends of a Wide panel are identical: each carries half a reveal. There is no lip to lead with and no overhang to
         tuck under. Butt the next panel straight up against the last one and the two halves close into a full 3 mm reveal
         that matches every other reveal on the wall. Keep the panels tight rather than leaving a gap to match the others
         — the spacing takes care of itself.

   Screws are not an option on this profile. The shadow reveal is 3 mm wide and every screw head is wider than that, so there is
   nowhere for one to sit. On the standard UltraLight Slat, where the groove is four times wider, trim-head screws do work — see that
   guide.

   Curves. Wide follows slight, broad wall curves — a shallow bow, a cove, a wide radius sweep. It will not wrap a column. The
   reveals are cut 1.5 mm into 9 mm, so the 7.5 mm backbone stays stiff; for a column or any tight radius use the standard UltraLight
   Slat instead, whose reveals are cut four times deeper and which bends much harder. Fix one end first and work along the curve,
   letting the panel take the shape of the wall — don’t flatten it out and try to bend it afterwards. Adhesive is what holds a bent panel
   to its radius.

  5         Work across the wall
            Butt each new panel up against the last and push until the two chamfers seat. The join should show the same
            3 mm shadow reveal as every other join — if it looks wider, the panels are not fully home. Check plumb every two
            or three panels.

  6         Openings & finishing
            Hold the panel up, mark the outlet or switch on its face, then cut the opening before you fix the panel. A jigsaw, a
            multi-tool or a sharp knife all work — the panel is 9mm of veneer and felt with nothing hard inside, and the cover
            plate hides the cut edges. At 0.35″ of added depth a standard plate usually still sits flat. A cut end shows the felt
            edge rather than end grain, so plan cuts to land behind baseboard, at the ceiling, or into a corner.

  8      Corners and edges
         The end chamfer on a Wide panel is 1.5 mm deep — a quarter of the depth of the one on the standard Slat — so it
         will not mitre itself at a corner. Each corner is handled on its own terms. At an inside corner, run one panel into
         the corner and butt the next square against its face; the factory chamfer leaves a shadow line and nothing needs
         cutting. At an outside corner, cut both ends at 45° through the full 9mm and butt them at the arris — the two
         veneer faces meet and the mitre itself is the finished edge, so no trim is needed.
