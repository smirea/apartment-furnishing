UltraLight Accent
White Oak · Walnut
Real wood veneer laminated straight onto 9mm recycled acoustic felt — no
MDF, no hardwood, no rigid core of any kind. Accent is the flat panel in the
range: a continuous veneer face, real wood wall to wall, with nothing cut into it.
The only line on the wall is the 3mm shadow reveal where two panels meet. This
guide covers what an installer or specifier needs to measure a wall, price the
job and put it up.

Panels are sold by the box, so work in boxes. Divide the wall width by what one box covers,

Start at a corner
Corners are what people look at, so start at one — inside or outside, whichever end of the wall is most
visible. Work away from it and let the cut panel land at the far end, ideally the end that dies into another
corner, a cabinet or trim. Accent panels are symmetrical end to end — both edges carry the same chamfer,
so there is nothing to orient. A cut edge, though, has no chamfer and will not make a full reveal, so plan the
run to let the cut panel's new edge die into the corner, trim or cabinet at the far end.

   No acclimation needed. There is no wood or MDF core to swell or shrink, so you can install the day the boxes arrive. Do still open
   several boxes and lay panels out before fixing — real veneer varies, and blending across the whole wall stops a patch showing
   where one box ends.

  1      Prep the wall
         Start with a clean, dry, flat wall. Take off outlet covers and switch plates and pull any nails or hooks. The panel is
         9mm and flexible, so it follows the wall rather than straightening it — fill or sand anything that would telegraph
         through. Adhesive needs a sound surface: scrape loose paint and let fresh plaster cure.

  2      Mark your layout
         Start at the corner shown on page 4. Draw a plumb line on the first panel’s leading edge — corners are rarely
         plumb, so trust the line, not the wall. Everything else follows it, so check it twice.

  3      There is no wrong way round
         Both edges of an Accent panel are identical: each carries the same 1.5 mm chamfer. There is no lip to lead with
         and no overhang to tuck under. Butt the next panel straight up against the last one and the two chamfers close
         into a full 3 mm reveal. Keep the panels tight — a continuous face shows everything, so a join that is not fully
         home reads immediately as a wide gap.

   Screws are not an option on this profile. The shadow reveal is 3 mm wide and every screw head is wider than that, so there is
   nowhere for one to sit. On the standard UltraLight Slat, where the groove is four times wider, trim-head screws do work — see that
   guide.

   Curves. Accent follows slight, broad wall curves along its length — a shallow bow, a cove, a wide sweep. It will not wrap a column.
   Nothing is cut into the face at all, so the full 9 mm of felt resists bending across the panel's width; for a column or any tight radius
   use the standard UltraLight Slat instead, whose deep grooves let it bend hard in both directions. Fix one end first and work along
   the curve, letting the panel take the shape of the wall — don’t flatten it out and try to bend it afterwards. Adhesive is what holds a
   bent panel to its radius.

  5         Work across the wall
            Butt each new panel up against the last and push until the two chamfers seat. The join should show the same
            3 mm shadow reveal as every other join — if it looks wider, the panels are not fully home. Check plumb every two
            or three panels.

  6         Openings & finishing
            Hold the panel up, mark the outlet or switch on its face, then cut the opening before you fix the panel. A jigsaw, a
            multi-tool or a sharp knife all work — the panel is 9mm of veneer and felt with nothing hard inside, and the cover
            plate hides the cut edges. At 0.35″ of added depth a standard plate usually still sits flat. A cut end shows the felt
            edge rather than end grain, so plan cuts to land behind baseboard, at the ceiling, or into a corner.

  7      Cut to fit
         Measure twice. A fine-tooth blade goes through the veneer face and a sharp knife through the felt — the face is
         11.69″ of unbroken veneer, far too wide for a knife alone. There is no groove to cut down, so a rip leaves a square
         edge with no chamfer: send that edge into a corner, behind trim or against a cabinet, never into a join in the
         middle of the wall. For height, cut from the bottom so the factory edge stays at eye level.

  8      Corners and edges
         The edge chamfer on an Accent panel is 1.5 mm deep — a quarter of the depth of the groove on the standard
         Slat — so it will not mitre itself at a corner. Each corner is handled on its own terms. At an inside corner, run one
         panel into the corner and butt the next square against its face; the factory chamfer leaves a shadow line and
         nothing needs cutting. At an outside corner, cut both ends at 45° through the full 9mm and butt them at the
         arris — the two veneer faces meet and the mitre itself is the finished edge, so no trim is needed.
